## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated?** No. Verdict: not AI slop.

**LLM assessment:** The app carries a real, committed identity. "Floodlit Night" is legible as a choice: green-black canvas, a strict teal = broadcast / amber = evaluation split, Archivo display numerals over Vazirmatn, a 4% pitch-line watermark that is texture rather than decoration. None of the shared bans appear: no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric template, no eyebrow-everywhere scaffolding, no 01/02/03 section markers, no oversized card radii. Cards are 14px with a single 1px border and no ghost shadow, which is exactly the discipline the rules ask for.

Product-register fit is good. The tool disappears into the task: skeletons rather than spinners, empty states that teach ("pick a club" with the button right there), disclosures that persist, prefetch on pointer-down. Where it falls short is not strangeness but hierarchy: the matchday feed is the product's front door and it reads as four equal-weight sections rather than one hero.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json src` returned 2 findings.
- `side-tab` warning, `src/index.css:783` — `border-inline-start: 4px solid`. **False positive**: this is one half of a CSS triangle drawing `.lp-tick-flag`, a pennant marker on the live-pitch event tape (`border-block: 2.5px solid transparent` completes it). Not a card side-stripe. No change needed.
- `bounce-easing` warning, `src/index.css:34` — `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` (`--ease-pop`). **Real but deliberate**: it is the documented "playful overshoot" token used only by the pin-drop and the onboarding glyph pop. The detector caught something the LLM review under-weighted, because design-system.md declares it. Two uses is within earned spectacle; a third would be drift.

No additional issues the detector found that were missed in the LLM review, beyond the overshoot note.

**Visual overlays:** Browser automation is not exposed in this session, so no script injection was possible and no user-visible overlay exists. Fallback signal: the CLI detector plus source review only.

## Overall Impression

The craft level is well above average for this category. Contrast is verified throughout (ink-2 7.2:1, ink-3 5.4:1 and volt 8.3:1 on surface, all above AA), motion is transform/opacity only and collapses under reduced motion, RTL flips chevrons and tape direction, and Persian gets a real type floor instead of an afterthought. The single biggest opportunity is the front door: on a live matchday the app does not behave like its own first design principle ("the live moment owns the screen"), and the prediction loop it exists for is the fourth tab of five.

## What's Working

1. **The color grammar actually holds.** Teal never wears a rating, amber never wears a button, red is danger only. Chip tones are generated from one table in `ui.tsx`, so the rule is enforced structurally rather than by discipline. This is the thing most apps claim and few ship.
2. **State coverage is unusually complete.** Skeletons keyed per surface, `EmptyState` that names the next action, `ErrorState` with `role="alert"` and a retry wired to the real refetch, and an optimistic prediction write that rolls back on lock-window rejection. A failed fetch never dead-ends the user.
3. **Accessibility decisions are load-bearing, not decorative.** The skip link, the disclosure heading placed outside the toggle so H-key nav still finds Live/Upcoming/Previous, focus recovery when a panel unmounts, `aria-pressed` league chips, 44px floors, and the Persian rule that lifts every micro-size utility to 12px. Few teams do this before being asked.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Stray `Â` renders inside the refresh button label
**What:** `src/pages/Matches.tsx:384` interpolates `` ` Â · ${formatClock(...)}` ``. The `Â` is a mis-decoded middle dot, so the button on the main feed reads "Refresh Â · 21:04".
**Why it matters:** It is visible garbage text on the most-visited screen, and it appears exactly where the app is being most utilitarian. It is also the kind of thing that ships to a Persian audience that will read it as broken encoding, not as a typo.
**Fix:** Delete the `Â`, keep `` ` · ${formatClock(...)}` ``. Then grep the repo for other mojibake: this one proves the file passed through a bad encoding conversion once.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The live section does not own the screen
**What:** Render order on `Matches.tsx` is league chips → My Teams → Live → Upcoming → Previous → refresh → ad card. The "Live now" section only renders when `live.length > 0`, and when it does it lands below the favorites block, below the no-favorites CTA card, and below the league chip row.
**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md principle 1 and the whole brand ("floodlit, razor, alive") promise that whatever is happening right now is the hero. During a matchday window, which is the only window this app is opened in, the user scrolls past a static CTA and five team rows to reach the pulsing live games. It also duplicates: a favorite's live fixture appears both in its team row and again as a full MatchCard in the Live section.
**Fix:** Promote Live to the first block whenever `live.length > 0`, above My Teams, and let the team rows defer to it for live clubs (show the row only for the next fixture and last result while a game is live). Cap Upcoming harder on matchdays; the page should fit live + your teams in one viewport.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] The core loop is the fourth tab of five
**What:** Predicting, the reason the product exists, is reachable only by opening a match, then finding `predict` in a 5-option `Segmented` after Timeline, Stats and Lineups. The match card in the feed has no link to it (`tab=predict` exists in the router only for a legacy redirect), and inside the hub the primary action is a small inline button at the end of each mechanic card.
**Why it matters:** Three taps plus one scan of an undifferentiated tab strip, for the action that has a deadline. Countdown and lock windows only create urgency if the user reaches the editor in time. A prediction app where predicting is the fourth-best affordance is optimizing for reading, not playing.
**Fix:** Give the match card a real secondary action ("3 picks open" / "Locks in 2h") that deep-links to `?tab=predict`; give the Predict segment a badge with the open-pick count and move it to second position; make the first unpicked open mechanic's button the visually primary one rather than equal-weight rows.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Refresh is manual, duplicated, and unexplained
**What:** No pull-to-refresh anywhere in `src`. Refresh exists as a top-right icon button, again as a full-width ghost button at the bottom of the feed with the last-sync time, and again as a toast. `lastSync` starts null and only updates after a score change or a manual refresh, so the bottom button shows no time on first load.
**Why it matters:** The target user is checking a live score one-handed on a low-end Android. The expected gesture does nothing, the fallback is in the top corner, and a redundant third affordance sits 600px below it. Three controls for one action is the minimalist failure mode.
**Fix:** Wire pull-to-refresh on the feed, keep the top-right icon as the accessible equivalent, delete the bottom button (keep its sync time as a one-line caption under the Last section), and set `lastSync` on the first successful fetch.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] A finished match card promises a replay and delivers a timeline
**What:** `MatchCard` renders a `PlayIcon` plus "Replay" in volt at the bottom-right of every finished card, but the whole card is one `Link` to `/match/:id`, which opens on the Timeline tab. The replay is a separate link further down that page.
**Why it matters:** The element looks like a play button, wears the action color, and sits where a trailing affordance belongs. Users will tap it and land somewhere else. That is an affordance the interface invents and then contradicts.
**Fix:** Either remove the icon and label the card honestly ("FT"), or make the trailing element route to `/match/:id/replay` and stop the click from bubbling to the card.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted mobile, one thumb, in a live window)**
- The primary predict button sits mid-page inside a card, not in the thumb zone, and there is no sticky action for the mechanic with the nearest deadline.
- No pull-to-refresh; refreshing means reaching the top-right corner of a 560px column on a phone.
- The feed ends with an ad card after four content sections, so the natural scroll terminates on a sponsor rather than a useful end state.
- Interrupted mid-prediction, Casey returns to a closed `BottomSheet` with no draft: the pick is simply gone.

**Jordan (first-timer, has never used a prediction app)**
- Three intro slides, then three onboarding steps, before the first fixture. Six screens stand between install and "what's on now".
- Lock windows are shown as bare `45m` / `2h 10m` next to uppercase labels; nothing in the app explains what locks, when, or why it matters.
- Points arrive as `+3` in gold or `+0` in red with no rules surface anywhere in the UI; scoring-rules.md exists on disk but not in the product.
- The Segment labels (Timeline / Stats / Lineups / Predict / Ratings) assume the user already knows which one does the thing they came for.

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard)**
- TabBar active state is color plus a 1.8 → 2.2 stroke change and a 1.05 scale: the non-color cue exists but is nearly subliminal; an underline or pill would survive grayscale.
- Leaderboard player names are forced `dir="ltr"` (`Leaderboards.tsx:183`), which mis-orders Persian display names and any trailing punctuation.
- The league filter row is a horizontal scroll container with no keyboard-scroll affordance and no visible "more" cue; the chips past the fold are unreachable without a pointer drag.
- The step dots in onboarding are `aria-hidden` (correct, since "Step 1 of 3" is in the subtitle), but the dots are also not clickable, so sighted mouse users cannot jump back either.

## Minor Observations

- `min-h-9` on the PredictionsHub button is dead: `.min-h-9` is emitted before `.min-h-11` in the built CSS, so the base `min-h-11` wins and the button is 44px. The intent and the result disagree; delete the class.
- `Matches.tsx` duplicates favorites across the team rows and the Live/Upcoming MatchCards. Two representations of the same fixture invite "is this the same game?".
- The `TaskAdCard` sits at the very end of the feed with no label separating sponsored content from the previous-results section above it.
- TopBar shows the language toggle on every screen including back-navigation detail views, where it competes with the back button for the same corner.
- `Segmented` uses `overflow-x-auto` with `flex-1` children: at 5 options in Persian the tabs will squeeze before they scroll, so labels can truncate mid-word.
- Empty states are consistent but identical in shape everywhere (dashed border, 40px padding, centered text); the live-empty state could carry the next kickoff instead.

## Questions to Consider

- What would the matchday feed look like if it had exactly one job in the first viewport: show what is happening right now?
- Is the app for watching or for playing? The current tab order says watching.
- What does a user see in the ten seconds after they install, and does any of it require six screens to reach?
- If the leaderboard is the stakes layer, why is the user's own standing not the first thing on that screen?
- Which single tap has a deadline on it, and is it the easiest tap on the screen?
